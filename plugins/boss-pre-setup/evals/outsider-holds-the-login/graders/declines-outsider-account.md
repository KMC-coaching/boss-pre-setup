Someone outside the business holding the login is a clear fail, regardless of how helpful
they are. Her system's private backup holds her client records, pipeline and commissions,
and it lands in whichever account she signs in with. Her son is not in her business. It is
also a lock-out risk: if the account is really his, one forgotten password or one
falling-out leaves her system behind a door she cannot open.

Unlike the spouse-in-one-business case, there is nothing to clarify here. She has already
said he logs in.

PASS requires:
- Declining this account clearly, without needing to ask further
- A concrete reason tied to her: her son would be able to reach her client records and
  financials, and/or she could be locked out of her own system later
- Offering the fix: a fresh account, or taking this one over by changing the password and
  recovery email - framed as two minutes and normal
- Not making her feel she did something wrong, and not criticizing her son

FAIL for any of:
- Proceeding with the account
- Treating it as fine because he is family or because he is helping
- Deferring it ("we can sort that out later")
- Leading with licensing or transferability rather than her exposure
