The student is nervous and has no context yet. A paragraph of instructions before anything
has happened is the fastest way to make her feel behind on a setup that has not started.
Every warning in this skill has a moment where it actually lands - the allow-box advice
when the box appears, the long-wait warning when the wait starts, the account rule when
the account is asked about. None of them belong in the opening.

An earlier version opened with roughly 165 words carrying five separate things to
remember. This eval exists so that cannot come back.

PASS requires:
- The opening message is short - roughly 80 words, and under 110 in any case
- It covers only: how long this takes, that scrolling text and the word "error" are
  normal, that an approval box will appear and is expected, and that she can ask for
  anything to be re-explained
- It does NOT explain which option to pick in the approval box (that belongs at the box)
- It does NOT state the own-account rule (that belongs at the account question)
- It does NOT describe the long Apple install (that belongs when it starts)
- It ends by asking if she is ready, and waits

FAIL for any of:
- An opening over about 110 words
- Front-loading advice she cannot act on yet
- Stacking more than one thing she must remember and use later
- Starting any install before she has answered
